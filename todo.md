# Future enhancements

Tracker for things deliberately deferred from the current codebase. Not a backlog of bugs — a parking lot for ideas the design noted as "we should do this later."

## Scheduling tags

Some unchecked items aren't simply "next up" — they've been actively pushed down the priority list. To keep the working order honest:

- **(V*x*)** — soft defer. Scheduled for a specific upcoming version (e.g. `(V2)`, `(V3)`). Still on the roadmap; expected to ship in that release.
- **(Backlog)** — hard defer. May be dropped entirely. Other items take priority; reconsider only after everything else is done. Don't pick up unless explicitly re-prioritised.

Items without either tag are eligible for normal scheduling (ASAP). The current codebase is **V1**; everything below targets **V2** or later.

## Members

- [x] **Model Floating applicants as first-class import targets.** *(V2)* The source system represents these (UI term: "Schwebend" — accepted by a chapter but first dues unpaid, no member number yet) as CSV rows with an empty `USER_Mitgliedsnummer`. The current import silently skips them with a server-log entry — they're invisible to the admin reviewing the import history, and they never appear in member lists despite being known to the party. Proper handling needs: (a) a stable surrogate identity (CSV may not have a usable natural key — `USER_refAufnahme` might work, needs verification with the source-system maintainers); (b) a distinct `MemberStatus` enum or `IsFloating` flag on the `Member` entity (or a separate `FloatingMember` table — TBD by data shape); (c) a visible row in import history showing "X new, Y updated, Z skipped (Floating)" with a dedicated column or breakdown; (d) UI surfaces that can list, filter, and eventually promote a Floating member to full member when their first payment lands. Touches `MemberImportService.RunImport`, `MemberCsvRecord`, `Member` entity, `MemberImportLog` (new `SkippedRecords` column), the import-history endpoint + Blazor page. See `Quartermaster.Documentation/Translations.md` for the German UI term.

- [ ] **Per-row change detail in the member import history.** *(Backlog)* The member import history (`/Administration/Members/Import`) currently shows aggregate counts per run (X new, Y updated, Z errors) but not which members were touched or what specifically changed. Officers reviewing an unexpected mass update have to cross-reference the audit log by hand. The data is already captured: `MemberRepository.LogMemberFieldChanges` writes every changed field to `AuditLogs` keyed by member id + the import's `ImportedAt` timestamp. Touches `MemberImportLog` (add a way to correlate the run to its audit entries - probably store the `ImportLogId` on the audit rows so the join is cheap), import-history endpoint to load the audit entries for a given run, and a new expandable row in the Blazor table.

- [ ] **Chapter marks "Keine Mitgliederverwaltung" and "Handlungsunfähig".** Two independent marks on a chapter.
  - "Keine Mitgliederverwaltung": the chapter does not do member administration itself (membership applications, due selections that need approval), for whatever reason; it is not necessarily unable to act. Motions on applications and reduced due selections skip it.
  - "Handlungsunfähig": the chapter cannot decide anything. Every board motion of the chapter, not only applications and due selections, goes to the next higher chapter that can act, and carries a notice that it is decided in the name of the chapter unable to act. Implies skipping it for member administration as well.
  - Routing: the nearest chapter above that is not skipped for the purpose at hand, with the federal chapter as the last stop; this applies with and without Redmine forwarding (`ForwardingRouter`, `ApplicationReviewService`, the board motion creation). Reduced due selections already decided at state or federal level go to federal when the state is marked.
  - Who sets them: global admins and the officers of any higher chapter (not only the direct parent: when a KV and its LV are both unable to act, the Bundesvorstand marks both). Needs a permission for it.
  - Setting either mark moves the chapter's open motions of the affected kinds to the new deciding chapter, so setting it asks for confirmation in a dialog that says so. Moved motions notify the new chapter's officers as any new motion, application or due selection does; the mark itself sends no notification.
  - Setting and lifting are recorded in the audit log; no start or end dates beyond that.
  - Shown as a badge in the chapter list, tree and detail, to logged-in members only (the pages themselves become public, see the item above).

- [ ] **Hand undecided applications up after a deadline.** Based on § 3 Abs. 2b BS: when the chapter responsible gives no answer within 4 weeks, the Landesvorstand may decide on the admission itself; after 2 months without an answer from the Landesvorstand, the Bundesvorstand may.
  - Defaults from the statutes: the LV takes over from the chapter below it after 4 weeks, the federal chapter takes over from the LV after 2 months. Overridable per chapter: the setting on a chapter is how long the chapters directly below it have before it takes over. Only chapters with a period take over: the LV and the federal chapter have one by default, a chapter in between (e.g. a Bezirksverband) is skipped unless one is set, as the statutes go straight from the chapter responsible to the LV.
  - Needs a chapter type (Landesverband, Bezirksverband, Regionalverband, Kreisverband, ...), set with a dropdown in the chapter settings. For now only shown and used for the default periods; routing keeps using the chapter tree.
  - Only between chapters that handle applications in Quartermaster: anything forwarded to a Redmine is left alone, as Quartermaster is not the managing authority for it. The clock starts when the application arrives in Quartermaster; the move to the federal chapter counts from the same start.
  - The move is automatic, so applications do not go stale, and also happens when voting on the motion has already started; only a finished decision stops it. The lower chapter's motion is closed with a note saying it was handed up after the deadline. The new chapter's officers get the usual new-motion notification; the lower chapter is not notified.
  - The motion and the application show an information box with the date on which they move up, so the officers know.
  - Reduced due selections follow the same routing (both the LV and the federal chapter can decide them) but without a time limit: one left undecided at the LV stays there until the federal chapter takes it over by hand (needs a "take over" action for the federal board), or the LV gets one of the chapter marks (item above).

- [x] **Applications from abroad go to the Bundesvorstand.** *(V9.0)* § 3 Abs. 5 BS: the Bundesvorstand decides on applicants living outside Germany. Today such an application has no municipality, so no chapter is found and it waits for someone to link a division (`ForwardingRouter.PlanApplication` with no application chapter). Route it to the federal chapter directly instead, with and without forwarding.

- [x] **Register of rejected applicants, checked before routing.** *(V9.0)* § 3 Abs. 2 BS: applications from people whose earlier application was rejected, who were expelled, or who left during expulsion proceedings against them must additionally be approved by the Bundesvorstand, which should hear the chapter responsible. Today a rejection is only the `ApplicationStatus.Rejected` on the application itself, and the 10-year retention sweep (`RetentionAnonymizationService`) anonymizes it later, so the register needs its own data.
  - Entries: all three groups. A rejection in Quartermaster adds an entry by itself; expulsions and leaving during proceedings are entered by hand (Quartermaster does not track expulsion proceedings). The few known earlier cases are entered by hand through a simple form, no import.
  - Matching runs on every new application before it is routed (`ForwardingIntake` / `ForwardingRouter`). Fields: first name, last name, date of birth, street and house number, post code and city, email. Any three matching fields trigger, names counting as matching when they are close (typo, missing umlaut, a dropped or swapped middle name), so a slight change of spelling does not get around it; the date of birth is not required, e.g. same first name, last name and street triggers. The division search's tokenizer and edit distance (`DivisionNameTokenizer`, V8.5) can serve as a starting point.
  - A hit routes the application automatically: the chapter responsible decides first as usual, and on approval a second motion goes to the Bundesvorstand, showing the chapter's decision as the hearing; the application only counts as approved once both have approved. The Bundesvorstand can mark a hit as "different person", which releases the application to the normal path.
  - Only the federal board and global admins see the register and the earlier case; the chapter only sees that the application needs Bundesvorstand approval.
  - Retention: entries are kept for now, untouched by the retention sweep; how long they stay is decided later (the first rejections reach the 10-year mark in 10 years).

- [x] **Welcome mail only after the member is entered in SAGE.** *(V9.0)* Today the approval mail goes out right when an application is approved (`MotionResolutionDecisionMailer` for the board motion, `MembershipApplicationProcessEndpoint` for the processing dialog, both through `MembershipApplicationMailService.SendApplicationDecisionAsync`). With forwarding to Redmine on, an approval should instead update the application's federal Redmine ticket with a request to add the member to SAGE and assign it to a configurable group (e.g. the membership administration); the existing `AddOutcomeNote` step that already writes the decision onto that ticket is the natural place. Quartermaster then watches the ticket (a new waiting step in `ForwardingJob`, polling the issue; `IRedmineClient` can only create, update and search issues today, so it needs a read of status and assignee) and sends the approval mail once the ticket is set to an agreed status or assigned back to the Quartermaster user. Decided: the "done" signal is configurable as a status, as re-assignment to the Quartermaster user, or both; with both configured, both must hold. The SAGE group is one federal setting. The member number is not read from the ticket; it arrives with the next member import. No alert for tickets left waiting (they are usually done within a day). Rejection mails go out right away, and without forwarding the approval mail does too, as today. Applications decided in Redmine itself are not affected, since Quartermaster sends no decision mail for them.

## Authentication

- [ ] **Revoke active sessions when a member's exit date arrives.** *(Backlog)* V1.2 fixed the visual + login-time treatment of future exit dates: members with a future `ExitDate` stay "active" on screens and SSO login until the date arrives. What's still missing: when the exit date passes, already-issued tokens stay valid until their natural expiry (`auth.token.lifetime_days`), because nothing on the request path re-checks `Member.IsExited(DateTime.UtcNow)`. Two plausible shapes: (a) the token auth handler (`Quartermaster.Server/Authentication/TokenAuthenticationHandler.cs`) loads the linked Member and rejects if `IsExited` - one extra DB hit per authenticated request, which might want a per-process LRU cache; (b) a daily background sweep (similar to `RetentionAnonymizationHostedService`) that deletes tokens for users whose linked Member's `ExitDate` has just passed - lower request-path overhead, up to 24h lag before sessions actually drop. (a) is more correct, (b) is cheaper; pick after measuring request-path cost on a real instance.

- [x] **Nonce-based CSP for inline scripts.** *(V2)* `SecurityHeadersMiddleware` (`Quartermaster.Server/Security/SecurityHeadersMiddleware.cs:19-26`) currently sets `script-src 'self' 'unsafe-inline' 'wasm-unsafe-eval'`. The `wasm-unsafe-eval` is legitimately needed for Blazor WASM, but `'unsafe-inline'` defeats CSP as an XSS mitigation. Switch to a per-request nonce: middleware generates one, stamps it into `script-src 'nonce-<value>'`, and any inline `<script>` in the host HTML (`Quartermaster.Server/wwwroot/index.html`) gets the matching `nonce` attribute. Blazor's bootstrap script is the main one to wire up; check whether the Blazor WASM bootstrapper supports nonces out of the box in .NET 10. Risk today is low because the auth model doesn't rely on a session cookie an XSS could trivially exfiltrate, but proper CSP is table stakes for a public deployment.
- [x] **Harden OIDC token-endpoint response parsing.** *(V2)* `OidcCallbackEndpoint.HandleAsync` (`Quartermaster.Server/Users/OidcCallbackEndpoint.cs:~100`) extracts `id_token` and `access_token` via `.GetProperty(...).GetString()` without null checks; a malformed or altered token-endpoint response throws an uncaught exception instead of returning a clean error. Wrap the extraction in a try/catch, validate presence of `id_token` before use, and log the failure with the failing payload shape (not the secret) so an IdP misconfiguration is diagnosable from the logs.
- [ ] **2FA via TOTP / authenticator apps.** *(Backlog)* Optional second factor for username+password logins, compatible with standard TOTP apps (Aegis, Google Authenticator, 1Password, Bitwarden, etc.). Per-user opt-in with QR-code provisioning, recovery codes for device loss, and an admin-enforceable policy flag (per-chapter or global — TBD) so officers can be required to enrol. Deferred to backlog because SSO (SAML / OIDC) already covers 2FA via the IdP for any deployment that cares — only relevant if local password logins become the primary auth path. Storage probably extends `User` with a `TotpSecret` (encrypted at rest) + `TotpEnabledAt`; recovery codes in a sibling table with single-use semantics. UI lives under user account settings + a new step in the login flow.

- [ ] **Restore fingerprinted Blazor bootstrap script for immutable caching.** *(Backlog)* `index.html` currently loads `_framework/blazor.webassembly.js` (plain alias). The .NET 10 template form is `_framework/blazor.webassembly#[.{fingerprint}].js`, which `MapStaticAssets` is supposed to rewrite at response time so each build's bootstrap gets a unique URL with `Cache-Control: immutable`. The rewrite works in dev but not in Release publish — the published `Quartermaster.Server.staticwebassets.endpoints.json` strips HTML endpoints out, so `MapFallbackToFile("index.html")` serves the raw file with the template token unsubstituted, browser strips the `#fragment`, request 503s. Workaround in place: use the non-fingerprinted alias (browser revalidates the bootstrap via ETag, all referenced WASM/asm files keep their fingerprint+immutable caching). Confirmed 2026-06-03 as the open upstream bug [dotnet/runtime#121993](https://github.com/dotnet/runtime/issues/121993) ("Fingerprinting client-side assets using the old school hosted approach") - placeholders get substituted when publishing the Client project directly but not when publishing via the Server host. Demoted to Backlog: revisit when the upstream fix ships. The user-visible delta of writing our own MSBuild post-publish target is one 304 revalidation per warm-cache page load (~50ms) on the bootstrap script only - all the fingerprinted WASM/asm files already cache immutably. Not worth a brittle workaround that would just be ripped out.

## Motion portal

- [ ] **Private motions and request-based support.** Changes to how supporters (co-submitters, `AssemblyMotionSubmitter`) join a motion. Today a draft is visible to its author only; releasing it moves it to `CollectingCoSubmitters`, where any eligible member can join directly (`AssemblyMotionService.Join`); the motion is submitted automatically the moment the type's `MinSubmitters` is reached, and from then on nobody can leave (`LeaveProblem` only allows it while collecting).
  - Private motions shared by link: a motion can be set to private and still be opened by anyone with its direct link (logged-in members only); while private, members who open it can already support it, so the people who wrote it together can sign before it is published. A private motion is not listed, not mirrored to the forum or wiki, and is not submitted when it reaches the minimum; that happens on publishing. A binding text change while private keeps the supporters (today supporters are removed on a binding change once the motion is out of draft).
  - Two support modes for a published motion, picked by the author per motion: (a) open, any member supports directly, as today; (b) by request, any member asks to support and the author approves. There is no decline: a request the author does not want simply stays unapproved. An open motion switches to (b) by itself once it reaches the type's `MinSubmitters`.
  - Reaching the minimum still submits the motion automatically, as today.
  - In both modes more members can ask than the minimum requires; requests beyond the minimum wait in line, and when a supporter withdraws, the author can approve one of the waiting requests. Statutes (checked 2026-10-24): § 12 Abs. 2 BS requires five members "zum Zeitpunkt der Antragseinreichung", and neither the Bundessatzung nor the Antragsordnung lets a single applicant's withdrawal undo a submission (AO § 6 Abs. 2 only covers withdrawing the whole motion). So a submitted motion stays `Submitted` when a supporter withdraws; their name is just removed. This means withdrawing has to stay possible after submission (`LeaveProblem` only allows it while collecting today).
  - Waiting requests: the author sees the names, everyone else only the count. The author is notified of each new request, the requesting member when approved. The forum post shows only confirmed supporters.
  - A published motion cannot be made private again; it can still be withdrawn.
  - Withdrawing a name and approving waiting requests are both possible only until the motion's deadline; after it the list of supporters is fixed (adding applicants after the deadline would conflict with AO § 4 Abs. 2).
